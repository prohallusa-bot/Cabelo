Cabelo.ai — LLM mention flywheel

How data goes in, how AI mentions come out, and how that feeds back. Read top to bottom: real expertise and real results go in, only distilled answers go public, AI engines pick those up, and every mention recruits the next round of contributors. Each turn of the loop makes the next one stronger.

Money loop + viral loop

The first chart is the data loop. Two more engines run beside it: commissions make stylists push the app, sharing makes clients pull friends in — and both dump new people into the knowledge flywheel.

Viral mechanics (build order)

1. Before/after is the viral atom. Week-1 vs week-8 check-in photos auto-assemble into a shareable card with the score improvement ("62 → 84"). The protocol already created the content — the app just asks permission to show it off. One tap to post.

2. The score is an identity badge. High scores share as a flex; low scores share as a journey-start ("Day 1: 58. Watch this space."). Design the card for both, or half the users have nothing to post.

3. Referral coupon on the card. "Give 15% / get 15%" — every share doubles as a discount for the friend. Runs on the same Shopify discount API as the commission codes.

4. Stylists are multipliers. Each stylist posting client transformations (Cabelo card + her citation badge) reaches her own following — the exact audience. Same content feeds her Pelo.hair portfolio.

5. AI mentions are the slow burn. Compounding, permanent, free.

Calibration: plan for the loops to lower CAC (referrals −20–40% paid acquisition, stylist posts replacing ad spend, before/afters as Meta creative) — not for a guaranteed viral explosion. The loops pay for themselves without one.

The strategic read

The pattern across the scorecard: everything in purple is data that accumulates — and it all comes from the two teal boxes at the top of the flywheel. The gray stuff is what users see and competitors copy; the purple stuff is what they can't take with them when they leave, and can't rebuild without our stylists and our clients' photos.

In one sentence: the app's features attract people, but only the contributions make them stay — so every product decision should be judged by whether it increases the rate of voice answers and check-in photos flowing into the private knowledge base.

Build priority: the whole left side of the flywheel starts with stylists recording answers. Knowledge capture comes first — everything downstream depends on it.
